PROJECT SYNOPSIS
FARMLINK
Digital Agricultural Procurement & Farmer Marketplace Platform
Project Name: FarmLink
Project Category: Agricultural Technology (AgriTech) / B2B Procurement Platform
Target Market: Farmers and Agricultural Produce Purchasing Companies
Business Model: Single-Company Procurement Marketplace
Platform Type: Responsive Web-Based Application
Initial Market: Kerala, India

1. Introduction
Agriculture plays an important role in India's economy, providing livelihoods to millions of farmers. However, selling agricultural produce remains a challenging process for many farmers, particularly small-scale farmers who depend on local traders, intermediaries and traditional market channels to sell their harvested crops.
Farmers often face difficulties finding reliable buyers, negotiating fair prices, identifying demand and coordinating the sale and collection of their produce. On the other side, businesses that regularly purchase agricultural products face challenges in identifying farmers, locating available produce, comparing quantities and managing procurement activities efficiently.
FarmLink is a proposed digital agricultural procurement platform designed to address these challenges by establishing a direct digital connection between farmers and a registered purchasing company.
The platform allows farmers to register, create profiles and publish details of their available agricultural produce, including product type, quantity, expected price, quality and availability. A registered purchasing company can browse these listings, review available produce and initiate structured price negotiations with individual farmers.
Unlike conventional online marketplaces that primarily focus on product listings and direct purchases, FarmLink introduces a structured negotiation and procurement process. Both farmers and the purchasing company can submit offers, propose counteroffers and mutually agree on the final purchase price and quantity without requiring a conventional unrestricted chat system.
Once an agreement is reached, the platform facilitates order confirmation, payment tracking, collection scheduling and transaction completion.
FarmLink aims to create a transparent, organised and accessible digital procurement environment that simplifies agricultural produce transactions for both farmers and purchasing businesses.

2. Problem Statement
The traditional agricultural produce selling process involves several challenges for both farmers and purchasing companies.
Challenges Faced by Farmers
Limited access to reliable and consistent purchasing companies.
Dependence on intermediaries to sell harvested produce.
Difficulty negotiating prices directly with buyers.
Lack of visibility into the actual demand for their agricultural products.
Challenges in finding buyers for specific quantities of harvested produce.
Limited transparency regarding purchase agreements and payment status.
Difficulty managing multiple produce listings and sales transactions.
Lack of organised digital records of previous sales.
Challenges Faced by Purchasing Companies
Difficulty identifying farmers who currently have the required produce.
Time-consuming communication with multiple farmers.
Lack of centralised information about available agricultural stock.
Difficulty comparing product quantities, locations and expected prices.
Inefficient price negotiation and procurement coordination.
Challenges in tracking purchase agreements, collections and payments.
Lack of a centralised procurement history and reporting system.
Proposed Solution
FarmLink provides a common digital environment where farmers can publish available produce and the purchasing company can discover, review and purchase agricultural products through a structured transaction process.
The platform aims to reduce manual procurement activities, improve communication through predefined actions, and maintain transparent records of negotiations, orders, payments and collections.

3. Project Vision
The vision of FarmLink is to establish a reliable digital connection between agricultural producers and purchasing businesses by simplifying the process of discovering, negotiating, purchasing and collecting agricultural produce.
The platform aims to make agricultural procurement more organised, accessible and transparent while giving farmers greater control over the selling process.
In its initial stage, FarmLink will operate with one registered purchasing company and multiple registered farmers. This controlled business model will allow the platform to establish a reliable procurement process before considering expansion to additional purchasing companies.
The long-term vision is to develop FarmLink into a scalable digital agricultural procurement ecosystem that supports multiple buyers, farmer organisations and a wider range of agricultural products.

4. Project Objectives
The primary objectives of FarmLink are:
Direct Farmer-to-Company Connection: Establish a digital channel through which farmers can directly offer their agricultural produce to a registered purchasing company.
Simplified Produce Listing: Enable farmers to publish available vegetables and other agricultural products with relevant information such as quantity, expected price, quality and availability.
Transparent Price Negotiation: Introduce a structured offer and counteroffer mechanism that allows both parties to negotiate and mutually agree on purchase prices.
Improved Procurement Efficiency: Help the purchasing company discover available agricultural produce from multiple farmers through a centralised dashboard.
Transaction Management: Organise the entire purchasing process, from initial offers and order confirmation to payment and collection.
Stock Availability Management: Maintain accurate records of available, reserved, purchased and remaining quantities to prevent duplicate sales.
Payment Transparency: Provide clear payment records and support payment before or after produce collection, according to the agreed transaction terms.
Digital Record Keeping: Maintain transaction histories, purchase records, farmer profiles and procurement reports.
Accessibility: Provide a simple and user-friendly platform that can be accessed by farmers through smartphones and by purchasing businesses through desktop computers.

5. Proposed Business Model
FarmLink follows a controlled B2B agricultural procurement marketplace model.
Unlike open marketplaces where multiple buyers and sellers interact freely, the initial version will operate with a single registered purchasing company and multiple registered farmers.
Main Participants
A. Farmers
Farmers are the primary suppliers on the platform. They can register, create profiles and publish details of their available agricultural produce.
Farmers have control over their expected selling prices and can accept, reject or counter the purchasing company's offers.
B. Purchasing Company
The purchasing company is the primary buyer on the platform.
It can browse agricultural produce listings from registered farmers, review product details, negotiate prices, purchase available quantities and coordinate collection and payment activities.
C. Platform Administrator
The administrator manages the platform and ensures that the marketplace operates in an organised manner.
The administrator is responsible for farmer verification, product category management, company account management, transaction monitoring, dispute handling and overall platform administration.
Business Model Structure
The initial FarmLink business model consists of:
One registered purchasing company.
Multiple registered farmers.
Individual farmer produce listings.
Direct negotiations between the farmer and purchasing company.
Digitally confirmed purchase agreements.
Organised payment and collection management.
FarmLink itself acts as the digital facilitator of transactions rather than functioning as the owner or reseller of agricultural produce.

6. Platform Structure and Domain Organisation
FarmLink will operate through a single primary domain with separate interfaces for farmers, the purchasing company and platform administrators.
The main website will primarily serve farmers, while the company and admin portals will be accessible through dedicated routes within the same domain.
This approach provides a unified platform while maintaining separate user experiences and access permissions.
6.1 Main Website – Farmer Portal
Proposed URL: farmlink.in
The main domain will serve as the primary entry point for farmers.
When a visitor accesses FarmLink, they will be introduced to the platform, its purpose and the opportunities it provides to agricultural producers.
The main website will include:
Home page.
About FarmLink.
How FarmLink Works.
Farmer registration.
Farmer login.
Contact and support.
Frequently asked questions.
The primary focus of the homepage will be encouraging farmers to register and publish their agricultural produce.
The farmer portal will provide a mobile-first experience, ensuring that farmers can comfortably access the platform using smartphones.
After logging in, farmers will be redirected to their respective dashboard.
6.2 Company Portal – Procurement Dashboard
Proposed URL: farmlink.in/company
The purchasing company will have a dedicated portal for managing agricultural procurement activities.
The company portal will not be available through public registration. Its account will be created and managed by the platform administrator.
The company portal will include:
Company login.
Procurement dashboard.
Browse agricultural produce.
Farmer directory.
Listing details.
Offer and negotiation management.
Order management.
Collection scheduling.
Payment management.
Procurement reports.
Company settings.
The company dashboard will be designed primarily for desktop and laptop usage, providing an organised interface for reviewing multiple listings, comparing prices and managing procurement operations.
6.3 Admin Portal – Platform Management
Proposed URL: farmlink.in/admin
The administrator will have a separate management interface for controlling and monitoring the platform.
The admin portal will include:
Admin login.
Dashboard and platform statistics.
Farmer registration and verification.
Company account management.
Product and category management.
Produce listing management.
Transaction monitoring.
Order management.
Payment monitoring.
Dispute management.
Reports and analytics.
Platform settings.
Administrative access will be restricted to authorised personnel.
6.4 Role-Based Authentication and Access
Although all three portals operate under the same primary domain, each user will have access only to the functions associated with their assigned role.
The platform will support three primary roles:
Role
Access destination
Farmer
Farmer dashboard
Company
Company procurement dashboard
Admin
Administrative dashboard

After successful authentication, users will be redirected to the appropriate dashboard based on their account permissions.
For example:
Farmers accessing company management pages will not be permitted to view them.
Company users will have access to all authorised farmer listings and procurement activities but not platform administration.
Administrators will have access to platform management functions.
This role-based structure will maintain separation between the three operational areas while keeping the entire platform under one unified domain.

8. Major Functional Modules
8.1 User Registration and Profile Management
The platform will provide separate registration and login facilities for farmers and authorised company users.
Farmers can create profiles containing their personal information, contact details, location and relevant agricultural details.
Farmer registrations may undergo administrative verification before listing privileges are enabled.
The company account will be created and managed by the administrator.
8.2 Agricultural Produce Listing Management
Farmers can publish the agricultural products they currently have available for sale.
Each listing will contain:
Product or vegetable name.
Product photographs.
Available quantity.
Measurement unit.
Expected selling price.
Product quality or grade.
Harvest date.
Availability date.
Farm location.
Additional product information.
Farmers can manage their active listings, update permitted information, withdraw unavailable produce and view their previous listings.
The platform will support two listing types:
Ready Stock: Agricultural produce that has already been harvested and is currently available.
Upcoming Harvest: Agricultural produce that is expected to become available on a specified future date.
8.3 Company Produce Discovery
The purchasing company will have access to a centralised dashboard displaying agricultural produce listings from registered farmers.
The company can:
Browse available products.
Search by product name.
Filter by location.
Compare quantities and prices.
Review product photographs.
View harvest and availability information.
Review farmer information.
Identify produce that meets its procurement requirements.
This module aims to reduce the time and effort involved in finding agricultural suppliers.
8.4 Listing View Tracking
Whenever the purchasing company views a farmer's listing, the platform will record the viewing activity.
The farmer will receive a notification indicating that the company has viewed the listing.
This provides farmers with visibility into buyer interest and helps them understand whether their produce is receiving attention.
The system will also maintain listing view records and timestamps.
8.5 Structured Price Negotiation
One of the primary features of FarmLink is its structured negotiation system.
Instead of providing a conventional unrestricted messaging system, the platform will use predefined negotiation actions.
The purchasing company can submit an offer specifying:
Proposed price per kilogram or unit.
Required quantity.
Total purchase value.
Offer validity period.
The farmer can:
Accept the offer.
Reject the offer.
Submit a counteroffer with a different price or quantity.
The purchasing company can then accept, reject or respond with another offer.
All negotiation activities will be maintained as transaction history.
For example:
A farmer lists 25 kg of tomatoes at ₹100 per kilogram. The purchasing company offers ₹80 per kilogram. The farmer counters with ₹90 per kilogram. If the company accepts, the platform records the agreed purchase price and quantity and proceeds to order confirmation.
This system provides a clear record of the agreed price and quantity while avoiding the complexity of unrestricted chat communication.
8.6 Order Management
When both parties agree on the purchase price and quantity, the platform will create a formal purchase order.
Each order will contain:
Unique order reference.
Farmer details.
Purchasing company details.
Product information.
Agreed quantity.
Agreed price.
Total purchase amount.
Payment arrangement.
Collection details.
Order status.
Confirmed quantities will be reserved against the farmer's available stock to prevent duplicate purchases.
The platform will also support partial purchases, allowing the company to purchase only a portion of the listed quantity while the remaining stock continues to be available.
8.7 Payment Management
FarmLink will support two payment arrangements.
Payment Before Collection
The purchasing company can complete payment after accepting the purchase agreement and before collecting the agricultural produce.
Once the payment is verified, the collection process can proceed.
Payment After Collection
The purchasing company can collect the produce, verify the actual weight and accepted quantity, and complete the payment according to the agreed terms.
The platform will maintain payment records, transaction references, payment status and relevant timestamps.
It will also support payment reconciliation and applicable refund records.
The actual fund settlement will be handled through an appropriate authorised payment arrangement or direct bank transfer, depending on the selected business model.
8.8 Produce Collection Management
After order confirmation, the purchasing company can arrange the collection of agricultural produce.
The collection module will allow the company to specify:
Collection date.
Preferred collection time.
Collection location.
Expected quantity.
Assigned collection personnel.
Additional instructions.
Once the produce is collected, the actual weight and accepted quantity can be recorded.
Both the purchasing company and farmer will be able to review the collection information.
Photographs, weighing records and collection confirmation can be maintained as supporting transaction evidence.
8.9 Notification Management
The platform will provide notifications for important marketplace activities.
Examples include:
Company viewing a farmer's listing.
New purchase offer received.
Counteroffer submitted.
Offer accepted or rejected.
Offer approaching expiry.
Purchase order confirmed.
Payment completed.
Collection scheduled.
Produce collected.
Transaction completed.
Notifications will help both parties remain informed about their transactions without requiring continuous manual communication.
8.10 Reports and Transaction History
The platform will maintain organised records of all agricultural procurement activities.
Farmers can view:
Active listings.
Previous listings.
Received offers.
Confirmed orders.
Completed sales.
Payment history.
Total quantities sold.
Earnings from completed transactions.
The purchasing company can view:
Total purchases.
Product-wise procurement.
Farmer-wise purchases.
Purchase quantities.
Transaction values.
Pending payments.
Completed collections.
Historical procurement reports.
The administrator can monitor overall platform performance and transaction activity.

9. Complete Operational Workflow
FarmLink will follow a structured workflow from farmer registration to transaction completion.
Step 1: Farmer Registration
A farmer registers on the platform, verifies their mobile number and submits the required profile information.
The administrator reviews and approves the registration.
Step 2: Produce Listing
After approval, the farmer creates a listing by selecting the agricultural product and entering its available quantity, expected price, photographs, quality and availability information.
The listing becomes available for the purchasing company to review.
Step 3: Company Discovers Produce
The purchasing company accesses the procurement dashboard and browses available produce.
It can search, filter and review individual listings based on its procurement requirements.
Step 4: Listing View Notification
When the company opens a listing, the platform records the activity and notifies the farmer that their produce has been viewed.
Step 5: Price Negotiation
The purchasing company submits an offer containing its proposed price and purchase quantity.
The farmer can accept, reject or submit a counteroffer.
Both parties can continue negotiating through predefined offer actions until an agreement is reached or the negotiation is closed.
Step 6: Purchase Agreement
Once the company and farmer agree on the price and quantity, the platform creates a purchase order containing the agreed transaction details.
The corresponding quantity is reserved against the farmer's available stock.
Step 7: Payment Arrangement
The purchasing company follows the payment arrangement selected for the transaction:
Payment before collection.
Payment after collection.
Step 8: Produce Collection
The purchasing company schedules the collection and records the actual quantity received.
The farmer is informed and can review and confirm the collection details.
Step 9: Payment Completion
The payment is completed or reconciled based on the selected payment arrangement and final accepted quantity.
Step 10: Transaction Completion
The order is marked as completed once the required collection and payment conditions have been satisfied.
Both parties can access the completed transaction in their respective histories.

10. Key Business Rules
The platform will follow specific business rules to ensure that procurement activities remain organised and transparent.
Only verified farmers can publish active produce listings.
Only the registered purchasing company can purchase produce through the initial marketplace.
Farmers retain control over their asking prices.
Both parties must mutually agree on the purchase price and quantity.
Every offer and counteroffer must be recorded.
An offer must have a defined validity period.
Confirmed purchase quantities must be reserved against available stock.
Partial purchases must be supported.
A listing should remain available when some stock is still unsold.
Listings with no remaining available stock should no longer accept new purchases.
Confirmed order terms cannot be silently modified.
Any change to actual collection quantity must be recorded and communicated to both parties.
Payment completion must be verified before the platform marks an order as paid.
Completed transactions must remain accessible as historical records.
Disputes and exceptional transaction changes must be documented.

12. Revenue and Business Sustainability Model
The initial FarmLink platform will be designed around a single registered purchasing company and multiple farmers.
Several revenue opportunities can be considered as the platform develops.
A. Company Subscription
The purchasing company may pay a recurring subscription for access to the procurement platform.
Possible plans could include monthly or annual subscriptions based on procurement volume, number of users and reporting requirements.
B. Transaction-Based Commission
The platform may charge a predefined service fee or commission on successfully completed transactions.
This model should be introduced only after establishing a clear understanding of farmer and company economics.
C. Premium Procurement Services
Additional services may be introduced for businesses requiring advanced procurement reports, supplier management, demand planning and specialised operational support.
D. Enterprise Solutions
The platform could eventually provide customised procurement management solutions for larger agricultural purchasing businesses and food-processing companies.
For the initial release, the priority will be establishing a reliable procurement workflow rather than maximising revenue through additional charges.

15. Expected Project Outcome
The expected outcome of FarmLink is a centralised digital agricultural procurement platform that simplifies the relationship between farmers and purchasing businesses.
The platform will provide farmers with a structured way to advertise their available agricultural produce, receive purchasing offers and negotiate prices directly with the registered company.
For the purchasing company, it will provide a centralised system to discover produce, manage suppliers, negotiate prices, confirm purchases, arrange collections and maintain procurement records.
Through its unified domain structure, FarmLink will provide separate, role-based experiences for farmers, the purchasing company and platform administrators.
By combining produce listing management, structured negotiations, order processing, payment tracking and collection management, the platform aims to improve the overall organisation and transparency of agricultural procurement.
The initial single-company operating model will provide a controlled environment for validating the business concept and establishing a reliable transaction process. Once successfully validated, the platform can evolve into a larger agricultural procurement network supporting multiple buyers, farmer organisations and a wider range of agricultural products.

16. Conclusion
FarmLink aims to address practical challenges in the traditional agricultural produce purchasing process by introducing a simple, structured and transparent digital solution.
Its primary focus is to establish a direct digital connection between farmers and a registered purchasing company, reducing the complexity associated with finding buyers, negotiating prices and managing agricultural produce transactions.
The platform goes beyond basic product listings by incorporating structured negotiations, purchase agreements, payment arrangements, collection coordination and transaction tracking.
Its unified web-based structure will make it accessible to farmers through smartphones while providing purchasing businesses with a dedicated procurement management interface.
By focusing on the actual requirements of farmers and purchasing businesses, FarmLink has the potential to become a useful digital tool for improving agricultural procurement operations.
The project will initially focus on a controlled single-company procurement model, with a long-term vision of expanding into a scalable digital agricultural supply and procurement ecosystem.
